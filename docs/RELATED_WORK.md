# Related work and attribution

Neural Light is a scene-specific rendering experiment using established ideas: store view-dependent appearance near a known surface, decode compact learned features, and correct a cheap rendered image with a small model. The October 10 Experimental C16 package documents one implementation and its visual result. It does not claim to invent neural textures, surface light fields or learned deferred shading.

## Close prior art

- **Surface Light Fields for 3D Photography** - Daniel N. Wood, Daniel I. Azuma, Ken Aldinger, Brian Curless, Tom Duchamp, David H. Salesin and Werner Stuetzle, SIGGRAPH 2000. Surface light fields associate outgoing appearance with surface position and view direction. The paper describes construction, compression and interactive rendering from photographs and scanned geometry. This is the closest historical framing for a directional surface bake. Neural Light uses known procedural geometry and synthetic labels rather than reconstructing photographed objects. [Author project page](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/slf/), [author-hosted paper](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/slf/papers/siggraph2000-wood-slf.pdf), [paper DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/344779.344925).

- **Baking Neural Radiance Fields for Real-Time View Synthesis (SNeRG)** - Peter Hedman, Pratul P. Srinivasan, Ben Mildenhall, Jonathan T. Barron and Paul Debevec, ICCV 2021. SNeRG bakes a trained volumetric representation into sparse voxel colors/features, composites them along rays, then decodes view-dependent color with a small per-pixel network. The shared idea is shifting expensive appearance computation into fitted data and a compact decoder. Neural Light's surface atlas and known geometry are different from SNeRG's sparse volumetric grid. [Author project page](https://nerf.live/), [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14645).

- **MobileNeRF: Exploiting the Polygon Rasterization Pipeline for Efficient Neural Field Rendering on Mobile Architectures** - Zhiqin Chen, Thomas Funkhouser, Peter Hedman and Andrea Tagliasacchi, CVPR 2023. MobileNeRF stores learned features and binary opacity on textured polygons, rasterizes them, and evaluates a small view-conditioned fragment network. It is close conceptual prior art for mesh-associated learned features and deferred neural color evaluation. The preserved Neural Light base uses software BVH visibility at low resolution; it is not a reproduction of MobileNeRF's learned polygons or ray-free rasterization pipeline. [Author project page](https://mobile-nerf.github.io/), [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.00277).

- **Random-Access Neural Compression of Material Textures (NTC)** - Karthik Vaidyanathan, Marco Salvi, Bartlomiej Wronski, Tomas Akenine-Moller, Pontus Ebelin and Aaron Lefohn, SIGGRAPH 2023. NTC jointly compresses a material's texture channels/mipmaps into learned features and a small per-material network, with on-demand GPU decoding. It is relevant to the compact atlas/decoder design. Neural Light's baked radiance includes fixed lighting and is not an albedo/BRDF texture set or NVIDIA's NTC codec. [Author project page](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/rtr/neural_texture_compression/), [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17105).

## Implementation credits and scope

The engine host is Quake3e, derived from ioquake3/id Software's Quake III Arena engine, with Quake3e's Vulkan renderer crediting Quake-III-Arena-Kenny-Edition. Keep its original notices and component licenses. The scene recipe uses Blender/Cycles as the physical reference renderer, and fitting scripts use NumPy/PyTorch. These are implementation dependencies, distinct from the research papers above.

The C16 screen-space correction is a scene-trained small convolutional phase head over the low-resolution corrected color. It is not a generic super-resolution model. Its weights can memorize appearance from native training labels; they do not establish that high-resolution detail was recovered from information absent from the input. Later JBU, geometry-coverage and FSRCNN experiments are outside this preserved package and do not change its reported result.

The comparisons and runtime numbers belong to this scene, native image sizes, selected frozen assets and measured implementation. They are not a benchmark ranking of the cited methods. In particular, the cost of a later handwritten shader implementation is not evidence that a literature architecture is inherently slow.
